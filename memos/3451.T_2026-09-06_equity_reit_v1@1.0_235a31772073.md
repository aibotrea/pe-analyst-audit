## Thesis

At ¥128,000/unit, the implied distribution yield of approximately 6.6% provides a meaningful spread over Japanese risk-free rates, supported by five consecutive half-periods of revenue growth from ¥3,157M to ¥3,521M. The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Monte Carlo simulation yields a 12-month sim return of ~8.1% with PGain of 81.8% and a strong CAPM alpha of 7.8%, reflecting the attractive yield spread relative to a negative-returning benchmark. Conviction is moderated to 3 (Moderate) reflecting elevated LTV of ~52.4%, execution uncertainty from the hotel-sector mandate expansion approved in July 2026, and structural headwinds from continued BoJ rate normalisation.

## Key Risks
- BoJ rate normalisation (policy rate trending toward 0.5–1.0%): higher funding costs compress the DPU spread and could trigger cap rate widening across Tokyo commercial real estate, pressuring NAV and distribution capacity.
- Hotel sector mandate expansion (per 3451.T 2026-07-21 臨時報告書): broadening into operational real estate introduces revenue volatility, operational complexity, and underwriting risk absent from the prior lease-based portfolio.
- Elevated LTV of ~52.4% leaves limited headroom to the J-REIT 55% convention limit; a downward asset revaluation or rising cost of debt could tighten covenant headroom and restrict capital recycling flexibility.
- Small market cap and low liquidity (daily turnover ~500–1,100 units at ¥128,000): limits institutional participation, creates adverse price-impact risk on entry/exit, and reduces pricing power in asset transactions.
- JPY/GBP currency basis embedded in computed beta (IASP.L is GBP-denominated): the beta of 0.415 and derived alpha of 7.8% partly reflect currency co-movement rather than pure property-market sensitivity, creating model noise in the CAPM inputs.
