## Key Risks
- BoJ rate normalisation accelerating faster than consensus: a policy rate above 1.0% would reprice floating-rate borrowings and compress J-REIT cap-rate spreads, directly reducing distributable income and NAV.
- Structural shift in Japanese retail demand: e-commerce penetration continues to erode anchor tenant sales in suburban formats, with department store and general merchandise tenants at risk of lease non-renewal.
- JPY appreciation risk: yen strengthening against USD/EUR reduces inbound tourism spending in urban retail assets, weakening tenant sales performance and rent revision prospects.
- Refinancing concentration risk: a significant portion of JRF's debt matures within 2–3 years; rising JGB yields increase refinancing cost and reduce DPU cover if spreads widen.
- Backtest calibration limitation: Phase 2 calibration is a directional signal; vintage discipline arrives in Phase 5. Macro signals (FEDFUNDS, credit spreads) were not available in stored APAC rates for Japan, limiting macro overlay precision.
