## Thesis

Press-reported DPU is at a post-split record, and tenant-turnover rent growth is strong. The ~4.7% annualised yield plus modest growth gives a 5.7% expected return. The OU Monte Carlo gives a 72% probability of a positive 12-month return, with a beta of 0.43 against IASP.L (currency-basis caveat applies). The numeric chain scores 4, and I apply a one-step downward gate override to 3 because leverage and distribution coverage could not be verified from the filing bodies. The 8% one-month price decline and recent equity issuance also argue for restrained sizing.

## Key Risks
- Rate risk: BoJ policy at 1.25% and rising JGB yields could compress P/DPU and raise refinancing costs. The 5.7% E(R) is only about 1.6 points above the 4.1% US T-bill rate, a thin spread.
- Unverified leverage and coverage: LTV and AFFO coverage could not be extracted from the truncated EDINET bodies, and DPU data comes from a news summary.
- Dilution and execution risk from the July 2026 public offering and the JPY 35bn asset-rotation programme, if acquisitions are not accretive or disposals crystallise NAV losses.
- Momentum and price risk: the price fell about 8% in the last month, which may reflect information not captured here. The IASP.L 5-year return of -5.0% is a weak market backdrop.
- Methodology limitations: beta is computed against a GBP-denominated benchmark (currency basis noise), and this is a directional analysis, not a formal backtest.
