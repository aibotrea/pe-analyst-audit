## Thesis

At a unit price of JPY 79,800, the estimated distribution yield of ~4.2% provides a positive spread over the BoJ policy rate of 1.25%, and the CAPM alpha of +6.7% (Category B, currency-basis caveat) signals meaningful expected outperformance of the APAC REIT benchmark. However, the IASP.L 5-year trailing return of -5.2% reflects a challenging broader APAC REIT environment, and BoJ normalisation at 1.25% introduces ongoing cap rate expansion pressure. The OU Monte Carlo PGain of 67.4% at a 12-month horizon, combined with the JPY 10bn unit buyback program, supports a moderate conviction score of 3 with a 5% maximum position.

## Key Risks
- BoJ rate normalisation beyond consensus path (above 1.5-1.75%) could trigger material cap rate expansion and compress NAV, reducing distribution yield attractiveness relative to JGB alternatives.
- JPY/GBP currency volatility introduces noise into the beta calculation (Category B), meaning CAPM alpha of 6.7% may overstate or understate true excess return; this is the primary model uncertainty.
- Tenant concentration risk: as a large-lot logistics REIT, the top 3-5 tenants likely represent a significant share of gross revenue; departure or lease non-renewal by a major 3PL or e-commerce tenant would materially impair DPU.
- High annualised volatility of 18.5% means realised return dispersion is wide; the 12-month standard deviation from Monte Carlo of 12.5% implies frequent deviations from the base case even if the thesis is directionally correct.
- Macro calibration limitation: Phase 2 calibration is directional only; vintage discipline is not yet implemented, so backtest-mode conviction scores are not formally validated against out-of-sample outcomes.
