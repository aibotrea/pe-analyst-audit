## 2. Themes of the quarter

**A rising July, then two falling months.** IASP.L gained 3.1% in USD from 12 July to the end of the month, then lost 1.5% in August and 4.8% in September. Over the whole window 85 of the 93 REITs in the universe fell in price, in all four markets. US short rates and credit spreads rose over the same period: the three-month Treasury bill from 3.71% to 4.03% and the high-yield spread from 2.69% to 3.12%.

**Both tracks fell behind the benchmark in the first four weeks, not in the sell-off.** Both started in cash on 12 July. To 9 August IASP.L rose 2.9%, Track A returned -0.4% and Track B +0.8%. From 10 August to 30 September all three fell by a similar amount: Track A -6.4%, Track B -6.3%, IASP.L -6.0%.

**Track A invested like a short-term trader acting on long-term research.** It ended the quarter 94% invested across 34 names, and it turned its capital over 4.3 times in under a quarter. Two-thirds of the positions it bought were sold again, after a median of 11 days, against research that estimates returns over twelve months. 102 of the 116 positions it sold were sold to bring the portfolio back inside its own concentration limits, not because the view on the name had changed. Transaction charges were 2.33% of capital, about a third of the quarter's loss, and most of the loss was realised (-6.35%). This will be a key area of review over the next quarter, as the model's decision framework is refined. How can we slow the velocity without impeding the ability to react to material events in the market?

**Track B invested like a selective holder that kept its losers.** The committee decided 126 of the 346 memos; 198 expired before it reached them, so its portfolio reflects the part of the research it had time to consider. It traded far less (turnover 0.8 times, charges 0.63%), held more cash (13%) and ran a more concentrated book (top five holdings 30% of NAV). Two-thirds of its positions were still held at quarter end and most of its loss was unrealised (-3.93%). Its average loss was 2.3 times its average gain. It also received more in distributions: 1.75% of capital against Track A's 1.26%.

**The gap between the tracks was smaller than the gap in their trading costs.** Track B finished 1.20 points ahead of Track A. The difference in transaction charges alone was 1.70 points. On the 101 memos the two decided differently, Track B came out $25,728 behind. On this quarter's evidence the committee's advantage came from trading less; there is no evidence yet that it chose better.

**Nothing yet separated the names that rose from the names that fell.** 26 of the 169 memos Track A bought had a positive outcome at quarter end (15%), against 16 of the 112 it rejected (14%). The specialist's average estimated probability of a gain was 73%. These are twelve-month estimates read after one falling quarter, so this is an early reading, not a verdict.

## 12. Method and data notes

### Method

- Period membership follows the UTC date of each decision. Positions are valued at the period-end close and that day's FX rate.
- Return is the change in NAV. NAV = capital + realised P&L + unrealised P&L + interest on idle cash + distributions. Neither track takes subscriptions or redemptions. The first quarter starts from the USD 10M capital base at inception; later quarters start from the prior period-end NAV. Lifetime returns are not annualised.
- All P&L is after the 25 bps charge on every purchase and sale. Distributions are gross of withholding tax and booked on the ex-date.
- The benchmark is IASP.L on a total-return basis with distributions held as cash, restated to USD; the figure in GBP is shown alongside. The book earns T-bill interest on idle cash and the benchmark does not.
- Position returns are measured on the original cost of the position, not the cost left after part-sales.
- Past holdings are rebuilt from the portfolio snapshot stored with every decision, so a closed quarter gives the same figures whenever it is run. P&L on a part-sale of a position still held is valued from the snapshot price; totals are tied to the stored lifetime figure.
- Turnover is the lesser of purchases and sales divided by the average of opening and closing NAV. It is not annualised.
- Market figures for the universe are price changes in local currency, before distributions, for names with a price within seven days of both dates. Macro series are as stored by the platform from FRED and the BIS. No cause is attributed to any market move.
- Rejected names are scored on price only, in local currency, against the benchmark price return.
- A Track A / Track B difference is measured in USD as each track's P&L on the name at period end, with zero for a track that did not buy.
- PGain is the specialist's probability of a positive return over twelve months. Results on predictions younger than twelve months are an early reading, not a calibration test.
